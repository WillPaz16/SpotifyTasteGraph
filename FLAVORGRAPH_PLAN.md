# A Music FlavorGraph: Failure Report and Reformulation

## Context

You asked for a music analog to FlavorGraph/Flavonomics/Epicure — a graph where genre, sound, feeling, history, and influence all connect, readable through your own listening but embedded in something population-scale. You asked me to be prepared to fail.

I am reporting three failures first, with measurements. Then the reformulation that survives them. The failures are not incidental — one of them is permanent and changes what this project can honestly claim to be.

---

## Part I — The Failures

### Failure 1: The "molecular layer" does not exist for your library

FlavorGraph's power comes from chemistry: 1,561 flavor compounds, objectively measured, complete for every ingredient. The obvious music analog is audio features (valence, energy, timbre, tempo). Every path to them is closed:

| Source | Status |
|---|---|
| Spotify `/audio-features` | Gated Nov 2024 for new apps. Yours was created today — permanently outside. |
| Spotify `preview_url` (compute our own via librosa/Essentia) | Killed Nov 27 2024. Returns null. We cannot obtain audio to analyze. |
| AcousticBrainz (7.5M recordings, open dump) | **Frozen June 2022.** Live API and submissions dead since. |

I measured the AcousticBrainz path end-to-end on your 12 most-played tracks, trying up to 8 MusicBrainz recording-MBID variants each:

```
JOIN RATE: 3/12 = 25%
```

The misses are not random. **47% of your tracks — 45% of your listening time — were released after AcousticBrainz froze.** Zach Bryan (2022), Sam Fender (2025), Bruno Mars (2026), Bad Bunny (2025), Ella Langley, SIENNA SPIRO. No amount of engineering recovers data that was never collected. Compounding it, MusicBrainz fragments one song across dozens of recording MBIDs (album/remaster/compilation/single) and AcousticBrainz only holds whichever variants users happened to submit — the same version-collapsing problem your own `PROMPT.md` flagged for Spotify URIs, but with a 25% ceiling instead of a parsing fix.

### Failure 2: Your personal co-occurrence matrix is not estimable

This one is mathematical, not logistical, and no data source fixes it.

FlavorGraph observes 6,653 ingredients across 1M+ recipes: **~150 observations per node**. Co-occurrence statistics are dense and reliable.

You have 72 tracks and 6 playlists: **~0.08 observations per node**. Estimating pairwise co-occurrence over 72 items requires O(n²) ≈ 2,556 parameters. The observable co-occurrence matrix from 6 playlists has rank ≤ 6. This is under-determined by roughly three orders of magnitude.

Even the Million Playlist Dataset wouldn't rescue track-level analysis (2.2M unique tracks / 1M playlists < 1 observation per node) — and MPD has been officially withdrawn from AIcrowd anyway.

**The unit of analysis was wrong.** A track is not the analog of an ingredient. An ingredient is a small-cardinality, high-reuse object appearing in thousands of recipes. A track appears in a handful of playlists. This was a category error in the original framing, mine included.

### Failure 3 (the permanent one): there is no behavior-independent substrate

This is the failure that matters, and I want to state it plainly rather than bury it.

FlavorGraph is scientifically interesting because **chemistry is independent of cooking**. Molecular composition is measured in a lab, not inferred from what people eat. That independence is what lets it predict pairings *nobody has ever tried* — the compounds vouch for a pairing the recipe corpus has never seen. Ahn et al.'s entire flavor-network hypothesis rests on this.

Every substrate still available to us for music is **generated by the same listening behavior it would be used to explain**:

- Last.fm tags — crowd folksonomy, written by listeners
- Last.fm similarity — derived from listening co-occurrence
- Wikidata genres — editorial, but assigned by humans reacting to the music
- Your descriptors — LLM/editorially generated

Audio was the only independent modality, and it is exactly what we cannot get. So a music FlavorGraph built today can describe and organize taste, but it **cannot make the one move that makes FlavorGraph novel** — predicting an unheard-of connection from a modality that knows nothing about listeners. Any "discovery" it surfaces is a recombination of existing behavioral signal, not an independent prediction.

Partial mitigations (weak, but real): Wikidata's genre DAG is editorial rather than behavioral, and release year is objective. Together they form a thin quasi-independent layer. I will use them, and I will not overclaim them.

**If the goal is the scientifically novel version, this project is blocked until an open audio-feature corpus exists.** What follows is the strongest honest thing buildable instead.

## Part II — The Reformulation

### What is actually isomorphic to FlavorGraph

| FlavorGraph | Wrong analog (what we tried) | Right analog |
|---|---|---|
| Ingredient (6,653) | Track (72) | **Artist** |
| Recipe co-occurrence (1M) | Your playlists (6) | **Last.fm similarity** — population co-occurrence, pre-aggregated over millions of listeners |
| Flavor compound (1,561) | Audio features (25% coverage) | **Tag / genre substrate** |
| — | — | Your play time = personal overlay on a population graph |

The key substitution: **we stop trying to compute co-occurrence from your six playlists and instead consume Last.fm's, already computed across its entire user base.** For a single user this is not a compromise — it is strictly more information than your own history contains.

Honest caveat: Last.fm's similarity is a black box (collaborative filtering blended with tags, undisclosed weighting). FlavorGraph's authors held raw recipes; we hold a third party's opaque estimate. That is a real epistemic downgrade and belongs in any writeup.

### The density argument (why this works and the last version didn't)

Measured on your current cache: 96 artists, 286 tags, 3.29% bipartite density, and **1,898 similarity edges of which only 139 point inside your library**. The current graph is 93% pointing outward — those 1,759 outward edges are not waste, they are the boundary of the global music graph, already touching your taste.

Crawling that boundary to ~15K artists:

```
15,000 artists × ~20 similarity edges  ≈ 300,000 edges  →  20 edges/node
FlavorGraph: 8,000 nodes / 147,000 edges                →  18.4 edges/node
```

Structurally equivalent density, and more nodes than FlavorGraph has ingredients. Measured throughput is 3.1 req/s at 2 calls/artist ≈ 3 hours, checkpointed and resumable.

## Part III — Build

New files in `analysis/`, reusing `enrich.py`'s `_get_with_retry`, `lastfm_similar`, `lastfm_tags` and `parse.py`'s `load_tracks`:

```
crawl.py     BFS expansion over Last.fm similarity from your 96 seeds
embed.py     heterogeneous random walks → word2vec → UMAP
atlas.py     the navigable map
data/universe/   checkpointed crawl cache (resumable, gitignored)
```

**Phase 1 — `crawl.py`.** Breadth-first from your 96 artists, priority-ordered by similarity-weight to seeds so the crawl deepens around your taste rather than drifting into K-pop. Checkpoints every 250 artists to `data/universe/`; re-running resumes. Records `depth`, `discovered_via`, and per-artist tags + weighted similarity edges. Hard cap 15K, respects 429s with the existing backoff.

**Phase 2 — `embed.py`.** The actual FlavorGraph contribution, adapted:
- Heterogeneous graph: `artist —similar→ artist` (weighted) and `artist —tagged→ tag` (bipartite)
- Metapath-constrained random walks: `A→A→A` (behavioral) interleaved with `A→T→A` (substrate bridging) — the direct analog of FlavorGraph's ingredient→compound→ingredient metapath
- Train `gensim` Word2Vec (skip-gram, negative sampling) on the walk corpus → 128-dim embeddings. At 15K nodes this is CPU-minutes, no GPU.
- Project to 2D with UMAP (cosine metric)

**Phase 3 — `atlas.py`.** The Epicure-style deliverable: a single interactive HTML map.
- Every artist a point in the projected space; regions emerge as genre/style territories
- **Your listening overlaid** — played artists sized by play time and highlighted; everything else is the surrounding possibility space
- Curated + LLM influence edges drawn over the map, so lineage is visible against the sound-space
- Hover: tags, meta-genre, play time, how you got there; click: nearest neighbours
- The empty regions adjacent to your territory *are* the discovery frontier, now geometric rather than a ranked list

**Phase 4 — vault.** `Taste Atlas.md` in `Taste Analysis/` with the map's findings, your territories named, and frontier regions as checkboxes feeding `Next Directions.md`. Existing note bodies untouched, as before.

## Verification (falsifiable — these can fail and I will report it if they do)

1. **Degree distribution.** Fit a power law to the crawled graph; report the exponent with a Kolmogorov–Smirnov goodness-of-fit. Ahn et al. found ~2.3 for the flavor network. If ours is wildly off or the fit is rejected, the "same topology as food" claim dies and I say so.
2. **Held-out link prediction.** Hide 10% of similarity edges, train the embedding without them, measure AUC recovering them vs. a degree-preserving random baseline. **If AUC < 0.7 the embedding is not capturing real structure** and the map is decoration.
3. **Known-lineage sanity.** Nick Drake / Bert Jansch / John Martyn / Tim Buckley should be mutual near-neighbours in embedding space — your curated graph says they belong together and it was built independently of Last.fm. If they scatter, the embedding is wrong.
4. **Personal coherence.** Silhouette score of your played artists as a set in the projection, against a random same-size subset. If your taste isn't measurably more clustered than random, the "your territory" framing is false.
5. Zero isolated nodes; crawl resumability verified by killing and restarting mid-run.

I will report each number rather than only the ones that flatter the result.

## What this will and will not be

**Will:** a population-scale, navigable map of ~15K artists with your listening situated in it; genre/tag/influence/era layered together; a geometric discovery frontier.

**Will not:** a system that predicts connections from an independent modality. That requires audio, audio requires an open corpus, and none currently exists that covers post-2022 music. If that changes, `embed.py`'s substrate layer is where it would slot in — the architecture leaves the socket open.
