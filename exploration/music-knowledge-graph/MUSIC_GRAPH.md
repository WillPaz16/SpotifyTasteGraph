## How To Read This

Each artist entry has:
- **Genre/tradition** — where they sit historically
- **Sound** — what it actually feels like
- **Key works** — starting points, not exhaustive lists
- **Connections** — who influenced them, who they influenced, who sounds adjacent
- **Your response** — what landed, what didn't, context
- **Story** — the cultural/historical narrative behind the music

Relationship types used in the JSON graph:
- `influenced_by` / `influenced` — direct lineage
- `sounds_like` — aesthetic adjacency (not lineage)
- `collaborated_with` — actual recorded collaboration
- `bridges_to` — serves as a gateway between genres
- `part_of` — belongs to a scene or movement
- `discovery_path` — how you got there in this exploration

## The Central Thread

**To Zion (Ms. Lauryn Hill feat. Carlos Santana, 1998)** is the seed of this entire exploration. You responded to:
1. The nylon-string guitar warmth (Santana's tone)
2. Lauryn's vocal delivery — raw, confessional, unhurried
3. The emotional weight of the lyric
4. The pace — nothing rushed

That response led in three directions simultaneously:
- **Neo-soul lineage** (Sam Cooke → Al Green → Lauryn Hill → SZA → Olivia Dean)
- **Flamenco / nylon-string guitar world** (Paco de Lucía → Camarón → Rodrigo y Gabriela)
- **British/Irish folk-soul** (Bert Jansch → Nick Drake → Van Morrison → John Martyn → Jeff Buckley)

## Genre Map

### Neo-Soul / Soul Revival
*The tradition you already knew, now with lineage context*

**Al Green** (1971–)
- Soul, Southern soul
- Sound: warm, unhurried, voice riding just above the groove
- Key works: *Tired of Being Alone*, *Let's Stay Together*
- Influenced by: Sam Cooke, Otis Redding
- Influenced: Lauryn Hill, D'Angelo, Bruno Mars (via D'Mile)
- Your response: Anchor track in *ex-factor at 2am*

**Sam Cooke** (1931–1964)
- Gospel-turned-soul, the original crossover
- Sound: the most naturally gifted voice in American music — effortless, warm, devastatingly controlled
- Key works: *(What A) Wonderful World*, *A Change Is Gonna Come*
- Influenced: Al Green, Otis Redding, Rod Stewart, every soul singer after him
- Story: First Black artist to own his masters. Murdered in 1964 under disputed circumstances. *A Change Is Gonna Come* was written after he was turned away from a whites-only motel.

**Ms. Lauryn Hill** (1998–)
- Neo-soul, hip-hop soul
- Sound: voice that moves between rap cadence and pure gospel, guitar-forward production unusual for its era
- Key works: *The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill* (Album), *To Zion*, *Ex-Factor*, *Killing Me Softly* (Fugees)
- Influenced by: Roberta Flack, Nina Simone, Al Green, Carlos Santana
- Influenced: SZA, Beyoncé, Rihanna (sonically), Drake (Nice For What samples Ex-Factor)
- Story: Recorded Miseducation while pregnant with Zion, under industry pressure to prioritize her career. Won 5 Grammys including Album of the Year — first hip-hop album to do so. To Zion specifically features Carlos Santana (nylon-string guitar), which is why it sounds unlike anything else on the record.
- Your response: Seed track for entire exploration. Loved the guitar warmth, vocal delivery, emotional weight, pace.

**D'Angelo** (1995–)
- Neo-soul
- Sound: voice as texture, warm vintage production, rhythm as the lead instrument
- Key works: *Brown Sugar*, *Untitled (How Does It Feel)*
- Influenced by: Marvin Gaye, Sly Stone, Prince
- Bridges to: Contemporary R&B (Bruno Mars via D'Mile)

**Erykah Badu** (1997–)
- Neo-soul, Afrocentric soul
- Sound: jazz phrasing, incense-and-vinyl warmth, voice that bends time
- Key works: *On & On*, *Didn't Cha Know*
- Influenced by: Billie Holiday, Nina Simone, Parliament-Funkadelic
- Your response: Liked, in *guitar warm enough to sleep in*

**Roberta Flack** (1969–)
- Soul, jazz-influenced
- Sound: intimate, piano-led, voice that never rushes
- Key works: *Killing Me Softly With His Song* (the original — Fugees covered this)
- Bridges to: Lauryn Hill (direct influence and cover lineage)

### British / Irish Folk-Soul
*The new territory — intimacy and guitar warmth from a different cultural root*

**Bert Jansch** (1943–2011) 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿 Scottish
- British folk, fingerpicked acoustic
- Sound: percussive, rhythmic fingerpicking — the guitar as complete orchestration
- Key works: *Needle of Death*, *Running From Home*, *Bert Jansch* (debut album, 1965)
- Influenced: Jimmy Page (Led Zeppelin), Paul Simon, Nick Drake, Johnny Marr
- Story: Recorded his debut album in one session in a friend's flat in Edinburgh for £60. It changed British music.
- Your response: Liked — the rhythmic momentum stood out

**Nick Drake** (1948–1974) 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 English
- British folk, chamber folk
- Sound: fingerpicked acoustic guitar with jazz-influenced chord voicings, voice barely above a whisper, string arrangements that feel like weather
- Key works: *Bryter Layter* (album — best entry point), *Pink Moon* (album — austere, bleak), *Poor Boy*, *Northern Sky*, *One Of These Things First*
- Influenced by: Bert Jansch, John Renbourn, jazz harmony
- Influenced: Mazzy Star (sound), Beach House, The National, every sad indie artist of the 2000s
- Story: Released three albums to near-total commercial indifference. Died of antidepressant overdose at 26, possibly intentional, possibly accidental. His music was rediscovered in the 1980s and is now recognized as foundational. A Volkswagen ad in 1999 using *Pink Moon* introduced him to a new generation.
- Your response: Liked *Poor Boy* (lighter, jazzy) and *One Of These Things First*. Interested in *Bryter Layter* as full album listen — **confirmed good call, best entry point**

**John Martyn** (1948–2009) 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿 Scottish
- Folk-soul, jazz-folk, proto-ambient
- Sound: Echoplex tape delay on guitar creating a liquid, swirling texture — voice that slurs and bends like a jazz horn
- Key works: *Solid Air*, *May You Never*, *Couldn't Love You More*, *Solid Air* (album)
- Influenced by: Bert Jansch, jazz (especially Miles Davis), blues
- Influenced: Beth Orton, David Gray, trip-hop producers (sampled extensively)
- Story: *Solid Air* (the song) was written for Nick Drake, who was sinking into depression. Drake died shortly after. Martyn spent his later life battling alcoholism but kept producing remarkable music.
- Your response: Highlights — *May You Never* (warm exhale), *Couldn't Love You More* (devastating closer)

**Van Morrison** 🇮🇪 Irish
- Irish folk-soul, Celtic soul, jazz-rock, mystical folk
- Sound: voice that treats syllables like a jazz improviser treats notes — he will repeat a word eight times and make each repetition feel different
- Key works: *Astral Weeks* (album, 1968 — essential), *Moondance* (album, 1970 — more accessible), *Into The Mystic*, *Sweet Thing*, *Veedon Fleece* (album, 1974 — Celtic mysticism)
- Influenced by: Ray Charles, Lead Belly, Irish folk tradition, John Lee Hooker
- Influenced: John Martyn, Bruce Springsteen, Bono/U2, Elvis Costello
- Story: *Astral Weeks* was recorded in two sessions in New York, 1968. Morrison was 23, broke, and had no contract after leaving his label. He recorded it with jazz musicians who had never heard the songs before. It sold almost nothing on release. It is now on every greatest albums of all time list.
- Your response: **Highlight** — Astral Weeks and Into The Mystic both landed. Deepest rabbit hole recommended.

**Sandy Denny** (1947–1978) 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 English
- British folk, folk-rock (Fairport Convention)
- Sound: voice like a cathedral — huge, warm, perfectly controlled
- Key works: *Who Knows Where The Time Goes*, *The North Star Grassman and the Ravens* (album)
- Story: Greatest female voice in British folk. Died at 31 after falling down a staircase. The only guest vocalist ever to appear on a Led Zeppelin record (*The Battle of Evermore*, Led Zeppelin IV).
- Your response: Appreciated — atmospheric and beautiful

**Richard Thompson** 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 English
- British folk-rock, Fairport Convention
- Sound: narrative ballads with virtuosic guitar — the most technically accomplished guitarist in British folk
- Key works: *1952 Vincent Black Lightning*, *Shoot Out The Lights* (album)
- Your response: Surprised and engaged — the narrative energy broke the atmospheric spell effectively

**Tim Buckley** (1947–1975) 🇺🇸 American
- Folk-soul, avant-garde vocal
- Sound: voice as pure abstract instrument — he extended vowels and notes past the point of comfort into something genuinely strange and beautiful
- Key works: *Song To The Siren*, *Starsailor* (album — experimental)
- Influenced by: Fred Neil, Van Morrison
- Influenced: Jeff Buckley (his son), Talk Talk, This Mortal Coil (who covered Song To The Siren)
- Story: Tim Buckley and Jeff Buckley barely knew each other — Tim was mostly absent from Jeff's childhood and died of a heroin overdose when Jeff was 8. Jeff spent his career in complicated dialogue with his father's shadow. Hearing them back to back is intentional.
- Your response: **Highlight** — Song To The Siren was haunting, otherworldly

**Jeff Buckley** (1966–1997) 🇺🇸 American
- Folk-soul, art rock, chamber pop
- Sound: voice of extraordinary range and emotional precision — can whisper and devastate in the same phrase
- Key works: *Grace* (album, 1994 — essential), *Lover You Should've Come Over*, *Hallelujah* (the definitive version), *Lilac Wine*, *Dream Brother*
- Influenced by: Tim Buckley (father), Van Morrison, Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, Led Zeppelin
- Story: One studio album. Drowned in the Mississippi River in Memphis, 1997, aged 30. Was working on his second album. *Grace* is now recognized as one of the greatest debut albums ever made.
- Your response: **Highlight** — *Lover You Should've Come Over* was the emotional peak of the playlist

### Dream Folk / American Atmospheric
*American cousin of British folk — hazier, more atmospheric*

**Mazzy Star** (1989–)
- Dream pop, dream folk, shoegaze-adjacent
- Sound: Hope Sandoval's voice recorded at a distance, David Roback's guitar warm and slightly out of focus — music that feels like memory
- Key works: *Fade Into You*, *Look On Down From The Bridge*, *So Tonight That I Might See* (album), *Among My Swan* (album — darker, better for late night)
- Influenced by: Neil Young, Velvet Underground, Nick Drake
- Your response: **Highlight** — immediately resonated. *Look On Down From The Bridge* came on randomly and caught your ear. *Fade Into You* as bookend worked well.

### Flamenco
*The nylon-string guitar in its original home*

**Paco de Lucía** (1947–2014) 🇪🇸 Spanish
- Flamenco, nuevo flamenco, jazz-flamenco
- Sound: the definitive flamenco guitar — precision, speed, and warmth simultaneously. The architect of nuevo flamenco.
- Key works: *Entre Dos Aguas*, *Almoraima*, *Zyryab*, *Friday Night in San Francisco* (live album with Di Meola and McLaughlin)
- Influenced by: Classical flamenco tradition, Brazilian music, jazz
- Influenced: Virtually every flamenco guitarist after him, Rodrigo y Gabriela
- Story: Brought jazz and Brazilian rhythms into flamenco without losing its soul. His collaboration with Al Di Meola and John McLaughlin (*Friday Night in San Francisco*, 1980) is one of the greatest live acoustic guitar recordings ever made.
- Your response: **Loved** — Entre Dos Aguas and Almoraima as pure guitar opener, then throughout *duende between two waters*

**Camarón de la Isla** (1950–1992) 🇪🇸 Spanish
- Flamenco cante, traditional and nuevo
- Sound: the greatest cantaor (flamenco singer) of the 20th century — raw, guttural, emotionally overwhelming
- Key works: *Como El Agua* (with Paco de Lucía and Tomatito), *Volando Voy*, *La Leyenda del Tiempo* (album — experimental, with rock band)
- Collaborated with: Paco de Lucía (most important collaboration in flamenco history)
- Story: His 1979 album *La Leyenda del Tiempo* scandalized the flamenco establishment by adding electric bass, keyboards, and rock arrangements. It's now recognized as a masterpiece.

**Vicente Amigo** 🇪🇸 Spanish
- Nuevo flamenco, contemporary flamenco guitar
- Sound: Paco de Lucía's melodic sensibility carried into the contemporary era — lyrical, precise
- Key works: *Ciudad de las Ideas*, *Vivencias Imaginadas*

**Tomatito** 🇪🇸 Spanish
- Flamenco guitar, nuevo flamenco
- Regular collaborator with Camarón de la Isla

**Diego El Cigala** 🇪🇸 Spanish
- Flamenco cante, flamenco-jazz crossover
- Key works: *Lágrimas Negras* (with Cuban pianist Bebo Valdés — flamenco meets Cuban bolero)
- Your response: Smooth bridge between flamenco vocal and accessible melody

**Niña Pastori** 🇪🇸 Spanish
- Nuevo flamenco, pop-flamenco
- Key works: *Tú Me Camelas*

**Rosalía** 🇪🇸 Spanish (contemporary)
- Flamenco-pop, experimental
- Key works: *El Mal Querer* (album — concept album built on flamenco structure, Grammy winner), *MALAMENTE*
- Bridges to: Contemporary pop, Bad Bunny (collaborated), experimental
- Your response: Cool but single-listen — too fusion/experimental for repeat. Appreciated intellectually more than emotionally.

**Rodrigo y Gabriela** 🇲🇽 Mexican
- Flamenco-metal fusion, acoustic guitar duo
- Sound: two acoustic guitars playing at metal speed with flamenco technique — percussive, ferocious, joyful
- Key works: *Diablo Rojo*, *Rodrigo y Gabriela* (album)
- You connected this to: Fuerza Regida's Marlboro Rojo — mariachi guitar vibe
- Your response: **Loved** — energetic closer that sold both playlists. The controlled chaos you love.

### Fado
*Portuguese tradition — the sound of saudade*

**Amália Rodrigues** (1920–1999) 🇵🇹 Portuguese
- Fado
- Sound: nylon-string Portuguese guitar (12 strings, cousin of the Spanish guitar) + voice carrying a lifetime of longing
- Key works: *Estranha Forma de Vida*
- Story: The queen of fado. *Saudade* — the Portuguese concept at fado's core — is an untranslatable longing for something you can't name or get back. When she died, Portugal declared three days of national mourning.
- Your response: Loved the back-to-back with Paco de Lucía — emotional one-two punch

### Bossa Nova / MPB
*Brazilian music — warm, jazz-adjacent, the nylon-string guitar in tropical form*

**João Gilberto** (1931–2019) 🇧🇷 Brazilian
- Bossa nova (inventor, with Antônio Carlos Jobim)
- Sound: whispered voice, barely-there guitar, jazz harmony underneath — the quietest revolution in music history
- Key works: *The Girl From Ipanema* (with Stan Getz, Astrud Gilberto, Jobim)

**Caetano Veloso** 🇧🇷 Brazilian
- Tropicália, MPB
- Sound: voice of extraordinary range and cultural reach — equally at home in folk, avant-garde, and pop
- Key works: *Cucurrucucú Paloma* (Mexican song covered in Spanish — the gut-punch track)
- Story: Exiled from Brazil by the military dictatorship in 1969 for his political music. Returned to become one of the most important artists in Brazilian history.
- Your response: Single-listen — powerful but sits you down. Not for repeat.

**Marisa Monte** 🇧🇷 Brazilian
- MPB, pop-soul
- Key works: *Ainda Lembro* (with Ed Motta)

**Natalia Lafourcade** 🇲🇽 Mexican
- Mexican folk-pop, MPB-influenced
- Key works: *Hasta la Raíz* (preferred), *Nunca Es Suficiente*
- Your response: Liked *Hasta la Raíz* significantly more than *Nunca Es Suficiente*
